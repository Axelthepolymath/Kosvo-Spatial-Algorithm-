# Kosvo Spatial Framework — Research Summary

## Overview

The Kosvo Spatial Framework introduces concepts for describing the relationship between entities, their positions, observation perspectives, and rotational movement patterns.

---

# Definitions

## Kosvo

**Kosvo** is an entity that obtains a position beside another entity while remaining separate from the entity itself. 

From a third-person perspective, the relationship between Kosvo and the primary entity can appear as a rotational arrangement.

---

## Third Perspective

**Third Perspective** is an observation point located away from the entity being analyzed. It allows the observer to examine the entity's spatial position and relationships from an external viewpoint.

Possible directional observations include:

* North
* East
* South
* West
* Other spatial orientations

From this perspective, movements or positions can be understood as rotations or changes in spatial arrangement.

---

## Loom

**Loom** describes movement occurring in a circular or rotational pattern around a reference point.

**Key concept:**

* Continuous circular movement.
* Maintains a relationship with a central reference entity.
* Represents rotational change in position.

---

## Beside

**Beside** defines a spatial relationship where an entity is located near another entity without:

* Being inside the entity.
* Being located directly on the entity.
* Becoming part of the entity.

It represents an external position with proximity and separation.

---

# Conceptual Relationship

The framework connects four main ideas:

| Concept           | Meaning                                               |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Kosvo             | External entity positioned relative to another entity |
| Third Perspective | External viewpoint used to observe relationships      |
| Loom              | Circular or rotational movement pattern               |
| Beside            | External position adjacent to an entity               |

Together, these concepts form a model for describing spatial relationships, observation, and rotational interactions between entities.
